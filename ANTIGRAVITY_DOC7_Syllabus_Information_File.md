# ANTIGRAVITY EDUCATION SYSTEM
## Document 7: Syllabus Information File
## For: Antigravity Content Engine, Quest Designers, Character Dialogue Writers, Question Authors
## Scope: CBSE + AP State Board | Classes 1–10 | Mathematics, Science, Physics, Chemistry

---

> **PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT:**
> This is the definitive reference for content creators building quests, questions, and character dialogue for Antigravity.
> For each topic: the official name, a plain-English description of what the concept actually IS, the key subtopics, the most common misconception children bring to it, and the game world it maps to.
> This document tells Antigravity HOW these chapters, topics, and subtopics should be mapped and implemented in a disciplined, educationally correct way.

---

## HOW CONTENT MAPPERS MUST USE THIS DOCUMENT

**For quest designers:**
Every quest maps to one PRIMARY chapter. The quest's narrative must make the primary chapter's concepts feel necessary — not like interruptions. A child should be solving the quest's problem and *naturally need* the chapter's concepts to do so.

**For character dialogue writers:**
Characters never explain concepts before a child has encountered them. Characters ask questions, express curiosity, and guide discovery. The subtopics in this document are what concepts the character understands and can explain — but they explain them AFTER the child has observed and hypothesized.

**For question authors:**
Each subtopic below is a concept node in the knowledge graph. Questions must be tagged to exactly one concept node. The misconception listed for each topic is the most important thing to design questions around — not just testing if the child knows the right answer, but probing whether they hold the wrong model.

**For the adaptive engine:**
The prerequisite relationships implied in this document (some topics must come before others) must be encoded as edges in the concept graph. No child should encounter a concept whose prerequisites they haven't engaged with.

---

## CONTENT DISCIPLINE RULES

1. **Every concept must connect to the real world.** If a concept cannot be grounded in a real observable phenomenon, the explanation is incomplete. Characters always show before they explain.

2. **Every misconception must be confronted, not just ignored.** If a topic has a known misconception, there must be at least one question designed to surface it — not to trick the child, but to give them the chance to confront and correct their own wrong model.

3. **Bloom levels escalate within every topic.** Every topic progresses: first encounter is Level 1-2, practice is Level 3, mastery assessment is Level 4-5. No topic is "mastered" based on recall alone.

4. **No content is created for content's sake.** Every question, every dialogue exchange, every quest segment must serve a specific learning objective mapped to a specific concept node.

## CLASS 1 MATHEMATICS

### TOPIC: SHAPES AND SPACE
**What this is:** The earliest introduction to geometry — understanding how objects occupy space, how they relate to each other, and what properties make a shape a shape. Not about naming shapes; about *experiencing* spatial relationships.

**Subtopics:**
- Inside/outside: Objects can be contained within or outside a boundary
- Near/far: Relative distance and position
- Top/bottom, left/right: Directional vocabulary
- Basic 2D shapes: Circle — no corners, one curved boundary; Square — 4 equal straight sides, 4 right angles; Triangle — 3 sides; Rectangle — 4 sides, opposite sides equal
- 3D vs 2D: A ball is a sphere (3D), a drawing of a ball is a circle (2D) — first seeds of this distinction

**Primary misconception:** A square is "more correct" than a rectangle (children often don't realize a square is a special rectangle). Address gently — this becomes important in Class 6.

**Game world mapping:** Counting Forests — The Shape Clearing. Characters: small animated shapes with faces. Circumflex makes a brief cameo in his youngest form.

---

### TOPIC: NUMBERS 1–100 (COUNTING AND PLACE VALUE)
**What this is:** Building genuine number sense — understanding that numbers represent quantities, not just names in a sequence. Children who can recite 1–100 may not understand that 47 is 4 groups of ten and 7 ones.

**Subtopics:**
- Counting objects (1:1 correspondence): Each object gets exactly one count; this is the foundation of all arithmetic
- Number names and symbols: The spoken name "seven" and the written symbol "7" both refer to the same quantity
- Ordering numbers: The number line concept — 5 comes after 4 because it represents one more
- Skip counting (2s, 5s, 10s): The foundation of multiplication
- Place value — tens and ones: 47 = 4 tens + 7 ones, not just "forty-seven"
- Before/after/between: Positional language for numbers

**Primary misconception:** Larger numerals contain larger digits — e.g., children may think 19 is smaller than 21 but larger than 20 because they see 9 > 0. Address with number line visualization constantly.

**Game world mapping:** Counting Forests — The Number Grove. Character: Ekan in his youngest, most playful form.

---

### TOPIC: ADDITION (Class 1-3 progression)
**What this is:** The concept of combining two groups into one. Not just the mechanics of adding numbers, but understanding WHAT addition means — it answers the question "how many altogether?"

**Class 1 subtopics:**
- Joining two sets: Physical combining of objects
- Number bonds (pairs that make each number to 9): 3+4=7, etc.
- Addition facts to 9

**Class 2 subtopics:**
- Adding two-digit numbers without carrying
- Addition word problems

**Class 3 subtopics:**
- Adding with carrying (regrouping): When the ones place exceeds 9, a ten is formed
- Addition of 3-digit numbers
- Mental addition strategies: Rounding, making tens

**Primary misconception:** Addition always makes numbers bigger. (Breaks when you add 0 or add negative numbers. Lay the foundation now: adding 0 doesn't change a number.)

**Game world mapping:** Counting Forests — The Joining Bridge. Ekan physically combines groups of objects in the animation.

---

### TOPIC: SUBTRACTION (Class 1-3 progression)
**What this is:** The concept of taking away, finding how many remain, or finding the difference between two groups. Subtraction has three meanings: take-away, comparison, and missing addend.

**Class 1:** Subtraction from groups up to 9
**Class 2:** Two-digit subtraction without borrowing
**Class 3:** Subtraction with borrowing (regrouping)

**Primary misconception (CRITICAL for all future math):** "You can't subtract a larger number from a smaller one." This is true in whole numbers, but false in integers. Plant this seed: "For now, in our number world, we don't have enough of those — but in Class 6 you'll discover a bigger number world where this IS possible."

**Game world mapping:** Counting Forests — The Separation Path.

---

### TOPIC: MULTIPLICATION (Class 2-5 progression)
**What this is:** Multiplication is repeated addition of equal groups. Before children memorize any table, they must understand what multiplication MEANS: 3×4 means "3 groups of 4" or "4 repeated 3 times."

**Class 2 subtopics:**
- Equal groups: What does 3 groups of 4 look like?
- Arrays: Organizing groups into rows and columns
- Skip counting as multiplication

**Class 3 subtopics:**
- Times tables 1–10 — learned AFTER conceptual understanding, not before
- Multiplication as area: A 3×4 rectangle has 12 squares inside it
- Commutativity: 3×4 = 4×3 (array model shows why)

**Class 4-5 subtopics:**
- Multi-digit multiplication
- Long multiplication algorithm
- Multiplication in problem contexts

**Primary misconception (CRITICAL):** "Multiplication always makes numbers bigger." This breaks when multiplying by fractions (0.5×8=4) and will confuse students for years if not addressed. When introducing fractions and decimals, explicitly connect: "Remember our multiplication rule? Let's see what happens when we multiply by half..."

**Game world mapping:** Operations Archipelago — Multiplication Mountain. Ekan's older, more energetic form.

---

### TOPIC: DIVISION (Class 3-5 progression)
**What this is:** Division has two meanings — sharing (distributing equally among groups) and grouping (how many groups of X fit in Y). Both must be understood. Division is the inverse of multiplication.

**Subtopics:**
- Division as sharing: 12 ÷ 4 = "share 12 among 4 groups, how many in each?"
- Division as grouping: 12 ÷ 4 = "how many groups of 4 fit in 12?"
- Relationship with multiplication: 3×4=12, therefore 12÷4=3 and 12÷3=4
- Division with remainders: 13÷4 = 3 remainder 1

**Primary misconception (CRITICAL):** "Division always makes numbers smaller." Breaks with fractions: 1 ÷ 0.5 = 2 (dividing by a half gives a bigger number). Address explicitly when teaching fractions.

**Game world mapping:** Operations Archipelago — The Sharing Shore.

## CLASS 4-5 MATHEMATICS

### TOPIC: FRACTIONS (Class 3-7 progression, most critical)
**What this is:** A fraction represents a part of a whole OR a part of a set. The denominator tells you how many equal parts the whole is divided into; the numerator tells you how many of those parts you have. Fractions are one of the most conceptually difficult topics in primary mathematics — and one where misconceptions are most harmful.

**Class 3-4 subtopics:**
- What a fraction is: Dividing a whole into equal parts
- Half, quarter, three-quarter: Visual models first
- Unit fractions: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5
- Fractions of a set (not just a shape): 1/2 of 8 apples = 4 apples

**Class 5-6 subtopics:**
- Equivalent fractions: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 (WHY, not just the rule)
- Comparing fractions: Same denominator (compare numerators); different denominator (find common denominator)
- Mixed numbers and improper fractions: 1½ = 3/2
- Adding and subtracting fractions with same denominators

**Class 6-7 subtopics:**
- Adding and subtracting fractions with different denominators
- Multiplying fractions (fraction of a fraction)
- Dividing fractions (the "flip and multiply" rule — and WHY it works)
- Fractions, decimals, and percentages as three representations of the same concept

**Primary misconceptions (MULTIPLE — each must be confronted explicitly):**
1. **"1/4 > 1/2 because 4 > 2"** — The larger the denominator, the SMALLER each part. This is the most common and most damaging fraction misconception.
2. **"Adding fractions: 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5"** — numerators and denominators cannot be added separately
3. **"Multiplying fractions makes them bigger"** — multiplying by a proper fraction makes the result smaller
4. **"A fraction is always less than 1"** — improper fractions are greater than 1

**Game world mapping:** Fraction Peaks — multiple zones (The Parts Palace, The Equivalent Exchange, The Mixed Number Mountains).

---

### TOPIC: DECIMALS (Class 5-7)
**What this is:** Decimals are another way to represent fractions — specifically, fractions whose denominators are powers of 10. A decimal is NOT a new type of number; it is fractions written differently.

**Subtopics:**
- Tenths and hundredths: 0.3 means 3 tenths = 3/10
- Decimal place value: The position of a digit tells its value
- Converting decimals to fractions and back
- Adding and subtracting decimals: Align the decimal point
- Multiplying by 10, 100, 1000: The decimal point moves

**Primary misconception:** "0.9 is bigger than 1" or "0.12 > 0.9 because 12 > 9." Decimal place value confusion is extremely common. The number line is the best tool for confronting this: where does 0.12 live? Where does 0.9 live?

**Game world mapping:** Fraction Peaks — Decimal Dunes.

---

### TOPIC: PERIMETER AND AREA (Class 3-8, fundamental distinction)
**What this is:** Perimeter is the total length of a shape's boundary (1D measurement). Area is the amount of surface a shape covers (2D measurement). These are completely independent — a shape can have the same perimeter but very different areas, and vice versa.

**Subtopics:**
- Perimeter: Sum of all sides, measured in linear units (cm, m)
- Area of rectangles: length × width, measured in square units (cm², m²)
- Area of triangles: ½ × base × height
- Area of circles: π × r² (Class 7)
- Same perimeter, different area: A 2×6 rectangle and a 4×4 square have the same perimeter (16) but different areas (12 vs 16)
- Composite shapes: Divide into known shapes, add areas

**Primary misconception (EXTREMELY common, persists through Class 10):** "Bigger perimeter means bigger area" and "same area means same perimeter." These are FALSE. Two shapes can have identical perimeters but completely different areas. This must be demonstrated with physical examples.

**Game world mapping:** Geometry Gardens — The Fencing Fields and The Area Arena.

---

### TOPIC: FACTORS, MULTIPLES, PRIMES (Class 4-6)
**What this is:** A factor of a number divides it exactly with no remainder. A multiple of a number is produced by multiplying it. Primes are numbers with exactly two factors: 1 and themselves. HCF and LCM are practical tools for working with fractions and ratio.

**Subtopics:**
- Factor pairs: Every number has factor pairs (12 = 1×12 = 2×6 = 3×4)
- Prime and composite numbers: Definition, Sieve of Eratosthenes
- Prime factorization: Every composite number is a unique product of primes
- HCF (Highest Common Factor): Largest factor shared by two numbers — used to simplify fractions
- LCM (Lowest Common Multiple): Smallest multiple shared by two numbers — used to add unlike fractions

**Primary misconception:** "1 is a prime number." It is NOT a prime — it has exactly one factor (itself), and prime numbers have exactly two factors. This matters because the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (unique prime factorization) requires 1 not to be prime.

**Game world mapping:** Algebra Citadel — Factor Forest. Character: Vector in early form.

## CLASS 6-10 MATHEMATICS (Higher topics)

### TOPIC: INTEGERS AND NEGATIVE NUMBERS (Class 6-7)
**What this is:** Extending the number line below zero. Negative numbers represent quantities below a reference point — temperatures below zero, debts, positions below sea level. The number line becomes the primary mental model.

**Subtopics:**
- Meaning of negative numbers: real-world contexts
- Ordering integers: -5 < -2 (further left on number line, so smaller)
- Adding integers: Same sign → add, keep sign; Different sign → subtract, keep sign of larger
- Subtracting integers: Subtracting a negative = adding a positive (why: remove a debt = gain)
- Multiplying integers: Rules for signs (positive × negative = negative; why)
- Dividing integers: Same sign rules as multiplication

**Primary misconception:** "−5 is bigger than −2 because 5 > 2." The number line must be the constant reference. Also: subtracting a negative number confuses almost everyone — the physical model (taking away a debt) is essential.

**Game world mapping:** Algebra Citadel — The Other Side of Zero (based on NCERT Class 6 new textbook chapter name).

---

### TOPIC: ALGEBRA — EQUATIONS (Class 6-10 progression)
**What this is:** Algebra is the language for expressing mathematical relationships using symbols. An equation is a balance — both sides represent the same quantity. Solving an equation means finding the value that makes the balance work.

**Class 6-7 subtopics:**
- Variables: A letter represents an unknown or changeable quantity
- Expressions: Algebraic expressions describe relationships (2x + 3)
- Simple equations: x + 5 = 12, find x
- Solving by balance method: Whatever you do to one side, do to both

**Class 8 subtopics:**
- Linear equations in one variable with rational coefficients
- Equations with variables on both sides
- Setting up equations from word problems

**Class 9-10 subtopics:**
- Linear equations in two variables
- Quadratic equations (ax² + bx + c = 0)
- Quadratic formula
- Arithmetic progressions

**Primary misconception:** "The equals sign means 'the answer comes next'" — many children think = means "compute this." This relational understanding gap causes enormous confusion when equations have variables on both sides. Must be addressed at Class 6 level.

**Game world mapping:** Algebra Citadel — multiple zones. Character: Vector.

---

### TOPIC: GEOMETRY — LINES, ANGLES, TRIANGLES (Class 6-10)
**What this is:** The formal study of shapes, their properties, and the relationships between them. Geometry at this level transitions from visual recognition to logical reasoning and proof.

**Class 6 subtopics:**
- Basic geometric concepts: point, line, line segment, ray
- Types of angles: acute (<90°), right (=90°), obtuse (90°-180°), straight (=180°), reflex (>180°)
- Measuring angles with protractor

**Class 7 subtopics:**
- Lines and angles: Complementary, supplementary, adjacent, vertically opposite
- Properties of triangles: Angle sum = 180°, exterior angle theorem
- Congruence of triangles: Conditions (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS)

**Class 8-9 subtopics:**
- Quadrilateral properties: Square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, trapezium
- Circle theorems: Angles in semicircle, tangent-radius relationship
- Pythagoras theorem: a² + b² = c²

**Class 10 subtopics:**
- Similar triangles: AA, SSS, SAS criteria
- Trigonometry: sin, cos, tan and their definitions
- Applications: Heights and distances

**Primary misconception:** "Angle size depends on the length of the arms." It does not — angle size is purely the amount of rotation, independent of arm length. Demonstrate with a protractor: a tiny drawing of a 90° angle and a large drawing of a 90° angle are the same angle.

**Game world mapping:** Geometry Gardens — multiple zones. Character: Circumflex.

---

### TOPIC: STATISTICS AND PROBABILITY (Class 5-10)
**What this is:** Statistics is the science of collecting, organizing, and interpreting data. Probability is the mathematics of uncertainty and likelihood.

**Class 5-7 subtopics:**
- Collecting and organizing data: Tally marks, frequency tables
- Representing data: Bar graphs, pie charts, line graphs
- Mean: Sum of all values ÷ number of values (the "fair share" interpretation)
- Median: The middle value when data is ordered
- Mode: The most common value

**Class 8-10 subtopics:**
- Mean of grouped data
- Cumulative frequency and ogive
- Probability: Classical definition (favorable / total outcomes)
- Experimental probability: From actual trials
- Compound events: P(A and B), P(A or B)

**Primary misconception:** "Average means most common." Average (mean) is the balancing point; mode is the most common. "The average family has 2.4 children" — no family has 2.4 children. This context helps.

## CLASS 1–5 SCIENCE (EVS / General Science)

### TOPIC: LIVING AND NON-LIVING THINGS (Class 1-4 progression)
**What this is:** The distinction between things that are alive and things that are not. Living things share characteristics: they grow, reproduce, respond to environment, require food and energy, excrete waste.

**Subtopics:**
- Characteristics of living things: Growth, movement (not always visible), reproduction, response, nutrition, excretion
- Non-living things: May move (fan, car) but are not alive
- Plants are living: They respond, grow, reproduce — but very differently from animals
- Differences within living things: Animals vs plants vs fungi

**Primary misconception:** "Things that move are alive." A car moves, fire moves — neither is alive. Movement is neither necessary (barnacles) nor sufficient for life.

**Game world mapping:** Living World — The Alive Arches (Class 1-4 zone).

---

### TOPIC: PLANTS — STRUCTURE AND FUNCTION (Class 3-7)
**What this is:** Plants are complex organisms with specialized parts — each part has a function. The most important concept is that plants MAKE their own food using sunlight (photosynthesis) — they do not get food from soil.

**Subtopics:**
- Roots: Anchor plant, absorb water and minerals
- Stem: Transport water and nutrients upward; support structure
- Leaves: Site of photosynthesis — absorb sunlight, absorb CO₂, release O₂
- Flowers: Reproduction — contain male (pollen) and female (ovule) parts
- Photosynthesis: sunlight + CO₂ + water → glucose + oxygen

**Primary misconception (CRITICAL, persists through Class 10):** "Plants get food from soil." Plants get MINERALS from soil (iron, nitrogen, etc.), but their FOOD (glucose) is made in the leaves through photosynthesis. Soil provides raw materials; the plant manufactures its food. This distinction must be confronted explicitly.

**Game world mapping:** Living World — The Leaf Factory. Character: A green leaf-shaped character with large expressive eyes.

---

### TOPIC: HUMAN BODY — SYSTEMS (Class 4-9 progression)
**What this is:** The human body contains multiple organ systems that work together. Each system has a specific function, but they are interdependent — the digestive system needs the circulatory system to distribute nutrients, which needs the respiratory system for oxygen.

**Class 4-5 subtopics:**
- Skeletal system: Bones, joints, types of joints
- Muscular system: Muscles work in pairs (one contracts, one relaxes)
- Digestive system: Mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine

**Class 6-8 subtopics:**
- Circulatory system: Heart, blood, arteries/veins/capillaries
- Respiratory system: Lungs, diaphragm, gas exchange
- Excretory system: Kidneys, urine

**Primary misconception:** "Blood is blue inside your body." Blood is red in arteries (oxygen-rich) and dark red (not blue) in veins (oxygen-poor). Veins appear blue through skin due to light absorption properties of tissue.

**Game world mapping:** Living World — The Body Labyrinth.

## CLASS 6-10 SCIENCE (PHYSICS TOPICS)

### TOPIC: MOTION (Class 6-10 complete progression)
**What this is:** The description and explanation of how objects move. This is the gateway to Newton's mechanics.

**Class 6 subtopics:**
- Types of motion: Linear (along straight line), circular, oscillatory, random
- Distance and measurement: Standard units, why standard units matter
- Uniform vs non-uniform motion: Constant speed vs changing speed

**Class 7 subtopics:**
- Speed: Distance ÷ Time
- Average speed vs instantaneous speed
- Distance-time graphs: Slope = speed; horizontal line = stationary

**Class 9 subtopics (complete kinematics):**
- Distance vs displacement: Distance is total path length; displacement is straight-line separation from start
- Speed vs velocity: Speed is magnitude; velocity includes direction
- Acceleration: Rate of change of velocity = (v-u)/t
- Three equations of motion (for uniform acceleration):
  - v = u + at
  - s = ut + ½at²
  - v² = u² + 2as
- Velocity-time graphs: Slope = acceleration; area under graph = displacement

**Primary misconceptions (multiple, all important):**
1. "Speed and velocity are the same." Speed is scalar (magnitude only); velocity is vector (magnitude + direction). Running in a circle at 5 km/h — speed is constant, velocity is constantly changing (direction changes).
2. "If an object is moving, there must be a force pushing it." (Aristotelian misconception — contradicts Newton's First Law.) An object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced force.
3. "Acceleration means speeding up." Acceleration is any change in velocity — slowing down (deceleration) is also acceleration (negative).

**Game world mapping:** Force Domain — The Motion Plains. Character: Newton in young form.

---

### TOPIC: FORCE AND NEWTON'S LAWS (Class 8-10)
**What this is:** Forces are pushes and pulls that change the state of motion of objects. Newton's three laws describe how forces affect motion — they are the foundation of classical mechanics.

**Class 8 subtopics:**
- Force as push or pull
- Effects of force: Can change shape, change speed, change direction
- Types of force: Contact (friction, normal, tension) and non-contact (gravity, magnetic, electrostatic)
- Pressure: Force per unit area (F/A)
- Atmospheric pressure: Weight of air column above

**Class 9 subtopics (Newton's Laws):**
- First Law (Law of Inertia): An object at rest stays at rest; an object in motion stays in motion (same velocity) unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. Inertia is resistance to change in motion.
- Second Law (F = ma): The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to its mass. More force = more acceleration; more mass = less acceleration from same force.
- Third Law (Action-Reaction): For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction — acting on DIFFERENT objects.
- Momentum: p = mv, Conservation of momentum

**Primary misconceptions (CRITICAL — Newton's First Law contradicts everyday intuition):**
1. "A force is needed to keep something moving." In everyday life, friction always decelerates objects, so we constantly push. In frictionless environments, no force is needed. Newton's 1st law is counter-intuitive precisely because friction is invisible.
2. "Action and reaction cancel each other out." They DON'T cancel because they act on DIFFERENT objects. A horse pulls a cart — horse exerts force on cart, cart exerts equal force on horse. They don't cancel; they act on different bodies.
3. "Heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones." Galileo disproved this. All objects in vacuum fall at the same rate (g = 9.8 m/s²). Air resistance causes the difference we see in daily life.

**Game world mapping:** Force Domain — The Newton Fortress. Character: Newton in full form.

---

### TOPIC: GRAVITATION (Class 9)
**What this is:** Universal gravitation explains why objects fall, why planets orbit stars, and why the moon orbits the Earth. Gravity is a force between any two objects with mass.

**Subtopics:**
- Universal Law of Gravitation: Every two masses attract each other; force = G × m₁ × m₂ / r²
- g (acceleration due to gravity at Earth's surface): ~9.8 m/s²
- Weight vs mass: Weight is the gravitational force on an object (W = mg); mass is the amount of matter
- Free fall: Object falling under gravity only (no air resistance)
- Orbital motion: Continuous free fall around Earth (why satellites orbit)

**Primary misconceptions:**
1. "There is no gravity in space." There is gravity everywhere in space — it decreases with distance but never becomes zero. Astronauts are "weightless" because they are in continuous free fall (orbit), not because there is no gravity.
2. "Weight and mass are the same." Weight depends on gravity and changes on different planets; mass is constant everywhere.

**Game world mapping:** Force Domain — The Gravity Realm.

---

### TOPIC: WORK AND ENERGY (Class 9)
**What this is:** Work is done when a force causes displacement. Energy is the capacity to do work. Energy is conserved — it changes form but is never created or destroyed.

**Subtopics:**
- Work: W = F × d × cos θ (force in direction of displacement)
- Work done = 0 when: force is perpendicular to displacement; no displacement occurs
- Kinetic energy: KE = ½mv² (energy of motion)
- Potential energy: PE = mgh (energy stored due to position in gravitational field)
- Conservation of energy: KE + PE = constant (in absence of friction)
- Power: Rate of doing work = Work/Time

**Primary misconception (CRITICAL):** "Energy gets used up." Energy is transformed, not consumed. When a ball rolls to a stop, its kinetic energy becomes heat (through friction) — the energy still exists, just in a different form. This connects directly to real-world misconceptions about fuel and batteries.

**Game world mapping:** Force Domain — The Energy Exchange.

---

### TOPIC: LIGHT (Class 6-10 progression)
**What this is:** Light is electromagnetic radiation that travels in straight lines in uniform media. At interfaces (mirrors, glass), it reflects, refracts, or both.

**Class 6-7 subtopics:**
- Light travels in straight lines: Shown by shadow formation
- Reflection: Light bounces off surfaces
- Laws of reflection: Angle of incidence = angle of reflection
- Plane mirror images: Virtual, erect, laterally inverted, same size as object

**Class 8 subtopics:**
- Curved mirrors: Concave (converging) and convex (diverging)
- Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
- Magnification: m = -v/u

**Class 9-10 subtopics:**
- Refraction: Light bends when crossing between media of different density
- Snell's Law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂
- Critical angle and total internal reflection
- Lenses: Convex (converging), concave (diverging)
- Lens formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f
- Power of lens: P = 1/f (in dioptres)
- Dispersion: White light separates into spectrum when refracted (prism, rainbow)

**Primary misconception:** "Refraction bends the ray — the ray curves." The ray does NOT curve continuously — it travels in a straight line in each medium and CHANGES DIRECTION only at the interface. The path is two straight line segments, not a curve.

**Game world mapping:** Force Domain — The Light Sanctuary. Character: Lumen.

---

### TOPIC: ELECTRICITY AND CIRCUITS (Class 6-10)
**What this is:** Electric current is the flow of electric charge (electrons) through a conductor. Circuits provide a path for this flow. Resistance opposes flow. Voltage drives flow.

**Class 6-7 subtopics:**
- Simple circuit: Battery, wire, bulb — current flows from positive to negative (conventional)
- Conductors and insulators: Copper, iron = conductors; rubber, plastic = insulators
- Switch: Opens and closes circuit
- Effects of current: Heating (filament), magnetic (electromagnet), chemical (electrolysis)

**Class 9-10 subtopics (complete circuit analysis):**
- Ohm's Law: V = I × R (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
- Resistance: Depends on material, length (longer = more resistance), cross-section (thicker = less)
- Resistors in series: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ (total resistance increases)
- Resistors in parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ (total resistance decreases)
- Heating effect: H = I² × R × t (used in fuses, heaters, filaments)
- Electric power: P = V × I = I² × R

**Primary misconceptions (MULTIPLE, all critical):**
1. "Electricity gets used up by the bulb." Current flowing through a circuit returns to the battery — it isn't "used up." What is used is energy (stored in the battery). Current is the same throughout a series circuit.
2. "The bulb closer to the positive terminal is brighter." In a series circuit, current is the same everywhere. Brightness depends on resistance, not position.
3. "More cells = more current always." In a series circuit, yes. In parallel, each branch receives the same voltage.

**Game world mapping:** Force Domain — The Circuit Complex. Character: Electra.

## CLASS 6-10 SCIENCE (CHEMISTRY TOPICS)

### TOPIC: MATTER AND ITS STATES (Class 6-9)
**What this is:** Everything is made of matter. Matter exists in three main states (solid, liquid, gas) determined by the arrangement and energy of particles. Changes between states involve energy transfer.

**Class 6-7 subtopics:**
- Properties of states: Solids (definite shape and volume), liquids (definite volume, indefinite shape), gases (indefinite shape and volume)
- Interconversion: Melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, sublimation
- Diffusion: Particles in gases and liquids move and mix
- Effect of temperature: Higher temperature = faster moving particles

**Class 9 subtopics:**
- Latent heat: Energy absorbed/released during change of state without temperature change
- Evaporation: Surface phenomenon; depends on temperature, surface area, humidity, wind
- Gases have mass: Air has weight — it's not "nothing"

**Primary misconception:** "Gas has no mass." Gas is matter and has mass (though very small compared to liquids and solids). Demonstrate: weigh a sealed container, then open and re-weigh — mass changes as gas escapes.

**Game world mapping:** Elemental Realm — The State Chambers.

### TOPIC: ATOMS AND MOLECULES (Class 9)
**What this is:** All matter is made of atoms — the smallest unit of an element that retains its chemical properties. Molecules are groups of atoms bonded together.

**Subtopics:**
- Atoms: Smallest unit of an element; made of protons, neutrons, electrons
- Molecules: Two or more atoms bonded (H₂O = 2 hydrogen + 1 oxygen)
- Atomic mass: Relative mass of an atom (C = 12 is the standard)
- Molecular formula: Shows which atoms and how many (H₂O, CO₂, NaCl)
- Mole concept: A mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number) — a "counting unit" for atoms

**Primary misconception:** "Atoms are the smallest possible particles." Atoms contain protons, neutrons, and electrons. Protons and neutrons contain quarks. There are smaller structures, but atoms are the smallest unit that defines chemical identity.

**Game world mapping:** Elemental Realm — The Atom Workshop. Character: Atomo.

### TOPIC: ATOMIC STRUCTURE (Class 9)
**What this is:** The internal structure of atoms — the arrangement of protons, neutrons, and electrons — determines the chemical behavior of elements.

**Subtopics:**
- Subatomic particles: Proton (+1 charge, in nucleus), Neutron (neutral, in nucleus), Electron (-1 charge, orbiting)
- Atomic number: Number of protons = identity of element
- Mass number: Protons + neutrons
- Electron shells: Electrons occupy shells (energy levels) — K (max 2), L (max 8), M (max 18)
- Valence electrons: Electrons in outermost shell — determine bonding behavior
- Isotopes: Same element, different number of neutrons (same atomic number, different mass number)
- Historical models: Thomson (plum pudding), Rutherford (nuclear model), Bohr (planetary model)

**Primary misconception:** "Electrons orbit the nucleus in circular paths like planets." The Bohr model (planetary model) is a useful simplification but is NOT accurate — electrons exist in probability clouds (orbitals), not defined circular paths. At this level, Bohr model is used, but students should be told: "This is a useful picture, not the full truth."

**Game world mapping:** Elemental Realm — The Atom Architecture.

### TOPIC: CHEMICAL REACTIONS (Class 7-10)
**What this is:** A chemical reaction involves breaking old bonds and forming new bonds, producing new substances with different properties from the reactants.

**Class 7-8 subtopics:**
- Physical vs chemical change: Physical = reversible, no new substance; Chemical = new substances formed
- Signs of chemical reaction: Color change, gas production, precipitate, heat change, light
- Types: Combination (A+B→AB), decomposition (AB→A+B), displacement (A+BC→AC+B), double displacement

**Class 9-10 subtopics:**
- Balanced chemical equations: Law of conservation of mass requires balancing
- Oxidation and reduction (redox): Oxidation = loss of electrons; Reduction = gain of electrons
- Corrosion: Oxidation of metals (rust = iron oxide)
- Rancidity: Oxidation of fats

**Primary misconception (FUNDAMENTAL — law of conservation of mass):** "Chemical reactions create or destroy matter." They DO NOT. The same atoms are present before and after a reaction — they are just rearranged. This is why equations must be balanced. Burning wood appears to destroy matter, but CO₂ and water vapor are released — if you could collect everything, mass would be conserved.

**Game world mapping:** Elemental Realm — The Reaction Forge. Character: Rexi.

### TOPIC: ACIDS, BASES, AND SALTS (Class 7-10)
**What this is:** Acids and bases are opposite types of compounds defined by their hydrogen ion (H⁺) or hydroxide ion (OH⁻) character. pH measures acidity/basicity. Neutralization produces salts and water.

**Class 7 subtopics:**
- Acids: Sour taste, red litmus, pH < 7; examples: HCl, H₂SO₄, vinegar
- Bases: Bitter taste, slippery feel, blue litmus, pH > 7; examples: NaOH, Ca(OH)₂
- Indicators: Litmus, phenolphthalein, turmeric — change color with pH
- Neutralization: Acid + Base → Salt + Water (H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O)

**Class 9-10 subtopics:**
- pH scale: 0-14; 7=neutral; <7=acidic; >7=basic
- Strong vs weak acids/bases: HCl (strong), acetic acid (weak) — degree of ionization
- Salt types: Normal, basic, acidic, double salts

**Primary misconceptions:**
1. "All acids are dangerous liquids." Citric acid (in lemons), carbonic acid (in fizzy drinks), and acetic acid (in vinegar) are acids that are safe to consume. "Dangerous" is a property of concentration and reactivity, not simply being an acid.
2. "pH 0 is 'no acid' and pH 14 is 'maximum base'." pH is a logarithmic scale — pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4. pH can exceed 0 and 14 in extreme cases.

**Game world mapping:** Elemental Realm — The pH Peaks. Character: pH the Balance Keeper.

### TOPIC: PERIODIC TABLE AND ELEMENTS (Class 9-10)
**What this is:** The periodic table organizes all known elements by atomic number in a pattern that reveals repeating chemical properties. Understanding the table means understanding WHY the pattern exists (electron configuration).

**Subtopics:**
- Historical development: Döbereiner's triads, Newlands' octaves, Mendeleev's table (predicted missing elements!), Modern Periodic Law
- Periods (rows): Elements in the same period have the same number of electron shells
- Groups (columns): Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons → similar chemical properties
- Metals, non-metals, metalloids: Properties and positions on the table
- Periodic trends: Atomic radius decreases across a period, increases down a group; electronegativity increases across a period

**Primary misconception:** "The periodic table is just a list of elements." The PATTERN is the knowledge — the position of an element tells you its electron structure, which tells you its bonding behavior, which tells you its chemical properties. The table is a map, not a list.

**Game world mapping:** Elemental Realm — The Periodic Palace.
